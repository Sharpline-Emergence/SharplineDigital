Why Your BC Business Needs More Than
Social Media

By P. Beal — 28 years in banking & financial planning, 19 years as a small business owner, Fraser Valley, BC

Quick Read — Key Takeaways
  • We reviewed 83 Fraser Valley small business websites. On phones, 18 of the 20 we speed-tested took longer than 3 seconds to show their main content.
  • Social platforms control your reach. Your website is the one digital asset you own outright.
  • AI search tools lean heavily on websites when answering “who’s the best plumber in Chilliwack?”
  • Website content keeps working for years. Social posts get buried within days.
  • The winning formula: a fast website as your foundation, with social media driving traffic to it.

If you run a small business in the Fraser Valley, you’ve heard the advice: “just get on Instagram,” or “a Facebook page is all you need.” There’s truth in it, because social media reaches people where they spend their time. But a business that lives only on social platforms is renting its presence from companies that can change the rules overnight. We wanted to know how local businesses are actually doing online, so we checked. Here’s what we found, and what it means for you.

01 / We Reviewed 83 Fraser Valley Business Websites

Between late August and mid-October 2026, we ran 83 small business websites through our audit tool. Most were from Chilliwack, Abbotsford and Mission, with a few from Hope, Langley, Agassiz and Aldergrove. They cover trades, farms, dental and health clinics, retail and local services. We’re not naming anyone; the point is the pattern.

One caveat up front: these are sites we looked at while searching for businesses we might help, so the sample leans toward sites with room to improve. Treat it as a snapshot of what we found, not a survey of every business in the valley.

Why 3 seconds matters. Akamai’s analysis of about 10 billion retail site visits found that 53% of mobile visitors leave a page that takes longer than three seconds to load.2 Nine in ten of the local sites we phone-tested miss that mark, and the typical one takes more than twice as long.

What surprised us. Two things. First, the privacy gap: nearly two-thirds of the sites collecting visitor information, through contact forms or Google Analytics, had no privacy policy we could find. BC’s Personal Information Protection Act expects businesses to be open about how they handle personal information, so that’s a gap worth closing.

Second, slow sites showed up on every platform: WordPress, Wix, Squarespace and hand-built alike. The platform matters less than whether anyone has tuned the site for phones.

02 / You Don’t Own Your Social Media Presence

Every follower on Instagram, every Facebook post, every TikTok video belongs to the platform, not to you. Algorithms change without warning, and organic reach shrinks until you pay to reach your own followers. Accounts get suspended, sometimes with no clear reason and no one to call.

Your website is different. You own the domain, the code and the content. No algorithm decides who sees it, and no platform can take it away.

“Social media should drive traffic to your website, not replace it.”

03 / AI Search Leans on Websites

Google’s AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity and Copilot now answer questions like “who does good bathroom renos in Abbotsford?” To answer, they draw mostly on websites with clear, well-structured information: services, service area, hours, pricing, reviews.

Some public social posts can be seen by these tools, but a Facebook page rarely gives them the structured detail a good website does. If your services and service area aren’t spelled out on a site you control, you’re leaving the answer to someone else.

04 / Local Trust Starts With a Professional Site

When a Fraser Valley homeowner needs a plumber or a physiotherapist, they don’t just want to find you. They want to feel confident hiring you.

Stanford’s Persuasive Technology Lab asked more than 2,600 people to evaluate real websites. The most common thing they mentioned when judging credibility was the site’s visual design, cited in 46.1% of comments.3 A dated or missing website creates doubt, even when your actual work is excellent.

A website also holds what a social feed buries: your hours, service area, process, pricing, testimonials and before-and-after photos, all in one place that stays put.

05 / Speed Helps, and Page Builders Make It Harder

Google uses Core Web Vitals, its measures of loading speed and stability, as part of how it ranks pages.4 Google is clear that great content matters more, but between two similar local businesses, the faster site has an edge. The bigger cost is the visitors who give up before the page loads, as our test showed.

Platforms like Wix, Squarespace and WordPress with visual page builders tend to load extra code to power their drag-and-drop editors. It can be optimized, but it often isn’t. A custom-coded site starts lean: no builder overhead, no stack of plugins.

06 / Website Content Compounds. Social Posts Don’t.

A social post gets most of its views in the first day or two, then disappears down the feed. A useful page on your website, such as a service description, an FAQ or an article like this one, can bring in search traffic for years.

Every useful page you add gives search engines more to find. Think of your website as the asset and social media as the amplifier: publish on your site, share it on social, and let the page keep working long after the post is forgotten.

“Your website is the asset. Social media is the amplifier.”

07 / The Winning Formula for Fraser Valley Businesses

None of this is an argument against social media. Instagram, Facebook and LinkedIn are great for visibility and staying top of mind. The point is the relationship between the two.

Your website is your home base. It’s where you control the message and make the sale. Social media is the road network that brings people there.

In our review, more than 4 in 5 local sites had clear room to improve. That’s the opportunity: a fast, clear site you own outright puts you ahead of the majority of local businesses in your trade.
