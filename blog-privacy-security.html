Your Privacy Is Not an Afterthought.
How We Protect Sensitive Information.

By Peter — 28 years in banking & financial planning, 19 years as a small business owner, Fraser Valley, BC

Quick Read — Key Takeaways
  • Sharpline sites are custom-coded and static, so there’s no admin login, plugin library or customer database for attackers to target.
  • We keep forms short and steer sensitive intake into systems built for it, not your website.
  • We never ask for your passwords. You grant access, and you can remove it any time.
  • You own your code, domain and accounts, and we tell you exactly which outside services your site uses.
  • No one can guarantee a website will never be compromised. We reduce the risk at every step and tell you the truth about it.

Before I built websites, I spent 28 years in banking and financial planning. In that world, protecting a client’s private information isn’t a feature. It’s the job. One careless email, one shared password or one unlocked file cabinet can end careers and break trust that took decades to earn.

I brought that mindset with me when I started Sharpline Digital. Whether you run a trades business, a counselling practice or a small shop, your website touches information that belongs to other people: your clients’ names, emails, phone numbers and sometimes much more. Here’s how I protect it.

“Most web projects start with colours and layouts. Mine start with what data the site will touch, where it goes and who can see it.”

01 / Why a Former Banker Builds Websites Differently

Banking teaches you to ask one question before anything else: what happens if this information ends up in the wrong hands? That habit shapes every decision I make, from how a site is built to how I handle your logins.

Privacy is part of the design from day one, not something bolted on after launch.

02 / Six Ways We Protect Your Information

  • Less to break into. Most small business sites run on platforms with a database, an admin login and a stack of third-party plugins. Each one is a door an attacker can try. Sharpline sites are custom-coded and served as static files, with no public admin page, no plugin library and no customer database sitting on the web server.
  • We only collect what you need. A contact form doesn’t need a birth date or a reason for seeking help. We keep forms short on purpose. For health and wellness practices, we steer sensitive intake away from the website entirely and into a proper practice-management system built for that job.
  • Encrypted by default. Every site we build runs on HTTPS, so information typed into your site travels encrypted between your visitor’s device and the server.
  • We don’t ask for your passwords. When I need access to your domain, Google Business Profile or email, I ask you to add me as a user with only the permissions the job requires. You can remove that access with one click, any time. Your passwords stay yours.
  • You own everything. Your code, your domain and your accounts are in your name. Nothing is locked inside an agency account you can’t see into. If you ever part ways with us, you leave with all of it.
  • Honest about third parties. Some features rely on outside services, such as a form processor, Google Analytics or an email provider. We tell you which ones your site uses and what they receive. If you’d rather not use one, we’ll build without it. We hold ourselves to the same rule: our own Privacy Policy lists the services this site uses and what each one receives.

We also follow the spirit of Canada’s privacy laws, including PIPEDA and BC’s Personal Information Protection Act: collect only what’s needed, say why you’re collecting it, and protect it once you have it. If you’re not sure what BC’s law asks of your own website, start with BC Privacy Laws and Your Website.

03 / Trusted Where Confidentiality Matters Most

Few professions take privacy as seriously as mental health care. We’re proud to have earned the business of Irineu de Oliveira, an established psychologist in Brazil, who trusted us to rebuild his professional website in both Portuguese and English.

For a practice like his, a website has to welcome new clients warmly without putting anyone’s privacy at risk. That means no sensitive details collected through web forms, and a clear path for clients to reach the practice through secure channels. The same standard applies whether you’re in Chilliwack or across the world.

04 / What No One Can Promise, and What You Can Do

Any web designer who promises a website can never be compromised is selling you something. No one can guarantee that. What we can promise is to reduce the risk at every step and to be straight with you about where your data goes.

Security is also a partnership. A few habits on your side make a big difference:

  • Turn on two-step verification for your email, domain registrar and Google accounts.
  • Use a password manager, and never reuse the same password across accounts.
  • Remove access for anyone, including past contractors, who no longer needs it.
  • Never send client health, financial or ID details by regular email.
